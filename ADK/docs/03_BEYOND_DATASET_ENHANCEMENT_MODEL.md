## 1. Executive Summary & Foundational Economic Motivation

Municipal small-business emergency relief programs confront an acute structural crisis when deploying public capital into dense, distressed urban ecosystems. In San Francisco’s **Central Market** corridor—spanning the Market Street commercial spine from 5th to 11th Street, the dense residential single-room occupancy (SRO) communities of the Tenderloin to the north, and the fragile commercial cross-streets of 6th and 7th Street leading into South of Market (SoMa)—a municipal Grant Officer managing a scarce **\$50,000 quarterly micro-grant tranche** typically faces between \$150,000 and \$300,000 in aggregated emergency funding requests.

When public administrators employ naive prioritization models, relief capital is systematically wasted across three major failure modes:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                           THREE CATASTROPHIC NAIVE TRIAGE FAILURES                         │
├───────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┤
│   1. Distress-Only Naivete    │   2. Raw Network Centrality   │  3. Leaf-Node Starvation    │
├───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┤
│ • Evaluates applicants solely │ • Ranks firms by unweighted   │ • Over-indexes on B2B edges │
│   by self-reported arrears or │   raw transaction connections │ • Assigns 0.0 cascade score │
│   days until bank exhaustion. │ • Funnels emergency public    │   to corner grocers, retail │
│ • Funds doomed "walking dead" │   grants to corporate tech,   │   pharmacies, and clinics.  │
│   firms with fatal burn rates │   autonomous vehicle fleets,  │ • Starves sole food/health  │
│   while starving viable hubs. │   and parking conglomerates.  │   providers in car-free SRO │
│ • Creates zero durable lift.  │ • Corporate leakage trap.     │   neighborhood tracts.      │
└───────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Distress-Only Naivete (The "Walking Dead" Trap)**: Allocating funds purely by self-reported intake distress or rent arrears funds non-viable enterprises whose structural deficits exceed the grant size by orders of magnitude. The grant buys a few days of delay before inevitable bankruptcy, while foundational local suppliers with acute, bridgeable shocks are allowed to fail.
2. **Raw Network Centrality Naivete (The Corporate Leakage Trap)**: Scoring businesses by unweighted graph degree or naive inter-industry linkages inadvertently recommends public relief checks to venture-backed software providers, autonomous vehicle operators, and national parking holding companies. These corporate entities masquerade as "high-centrality neighborhood hubs" in public tax registries due to broad industry classification rollups, while possessing tens of millions in private capital.
3. **Leaf-Node Starvation Trap**: Pure inter-industry supply models systematically penalize terminal consumer storefronts. Because retail supermarkets, pharmacies, community legal centers, and neighborhood clinics sell to end-use human households rather than intermediate commercial businesses, they possess zero outgoing business-to-business (B2B) commercial edges. Naive models assign them an economic importance score of zero, starving the sole sources of fresh food, medicine, and legal support in high-poverty, car-free Census tracts.

## 2. Dimension 1: Financial Buffer & Consecutive Bad Quarters Runway Model

### 2.1 Baseline Logistic Survival Model

Let $r_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ denote the operational financial runway of applicant business $i$, defined as the number of consecutive bad quarters the firm can endure under current depressed revenue conditions before liquid cash reserves are completely exhausted.

Let $\kappa_i > 0$ denote the net quarterly cash burn rate of firm $i$ (in dollars per quarter):

$$\kappa_i = \text{Operating Expenses}_i - \text{Depressed Operating Revenues}_i$$

Given unassisted liquid reserves $C_i$, the applicant's runway is $r_i = C_i / \kappa_i$.

We model the baseline survival probability of firm $i$ over an annual policy horizon ($T = 4$ quarters) using a continuous, parameterized sigmoid survival response function $P_0(r_i) \in (0, 1)$:

$$P_0(r_i) = \sigma\big(k (r_i - r^*)\big) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(-k (r_i - r^*)\right)}$$

where:
- $r^* > 0$ represents the **critical inflection threshold** (median survival transition). In urban small-business operations, $r^* = 1.0$ quarter (approximately 90 days of cash reserves), marking the structural boundary between precarious paycheck-to-paycheck insolvency and sustainable liquidity.
- $k > 0$ is the **logistic steepness parameter** governing the sensitivity of survival to incremental liquidity. Calibrated against small enterprise cash-flow studies, $k = 2.0$.

```
      1.0 ┼                                              ╭─────────── Self-Insuring Zone (r ≥ 4.0)
```

### 2.2 Post-Grant Survival Dose-Response Function

When applicant $i$ receives an emergency municipal grant of amount $g_i \in [0, R_i]$, where $R_i$ is the verified grant request, the cash injection extends operational runway by:

$$\Delta r_i(g_i) = \frac{g_i}{\kappa_i} = \left(\frac{g_i}{R_i}\right) \Delta r_i^{\max}$$

where $\Delta r_i^{\max} = R_i / \kappa_i$ is the maximum runway extension under full funding. For typical micro-grants in Central Market ($R_i \in [\$5,000, \$10,000]$ against typical quarterly deficits $\kappa_i \in [\$8,000, \$15,000]$), $\Delta r_i^{\max} \in [0.5, 1.25]$ quarters.

The post-grant survival probability is:

$$P(g_i \mid r_i) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(-k \left(r_i + \Delta r_i(g_i) - r^*\right)\right)}$$

The **Marginal Survival Lift** $\Delta P(g_i, r_i)$ achieved by the grant is:

$$\Delta P(g_i, r_i) = P(g_i \mid r_i) - P_0(r_i) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(-k (r_i + \Delta r_i - r^*)\right)} - \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(-k (r_i - r^*)\right)}$$

### 2.3 Theorem 1 & Formal Proof: Marginal Survival Lift Maximization

#### Statement of Theorem 1
*Let the survival function $P(r) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-k(r - r^*)}}$ be twice continuously differentiable on $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ with steepness $k > 0$ and inflection midpoint $r^* > 0$. For any infinitesimal grant perturbation $dg > 0$ yielding runway extension $dr = dg / \kappa > 0$, the marginal survival efficiency:*

$$\psi(r) \equiv \frac{\partial P}{\partial r}$$

*achieves its unique global maximum at $r = r^*$. Furthermore, for any finite grant producing runway extension $\Delta r \in (0, 1.0]$, the finite marginal survival lift $\Delta P(\Delta r, r) = P(r + \Delta r) - P(r)$ is strictly maximized at:*

$$r_{\max} = r^* - \frac{\Delta r}{2}$$

*which lies strictly inside the viable paycheck-to-paycheck corridor $r \in [0.5, 1.5]$.*

#### Formal Mathematical Proof

**Step 1: First Derivative of the Logistic Survival Function**  
Let $u(r) = k(r - r^*)$. By the chain rule:

$$\frac{dP}{dr} = \frac{d}{dr} \left(1 + e^{-u}\right)^{-1} = -\left(1 + e^{-u}\right)^{-2} \cdot \left(-k e^{-u}\right) = k \cdot \frac{e^{-u}}{\left(1 + e^{-u}\right)^2}$$

Recognizing that $P(r) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-u}}$ and $1 - P(r) = \frac{e^{-u}}{1 + e^{-u}}$, we express the derivative in standard autonomous form:

$$\psi(r) = \frac{dP}{dr} = k P(r) \big(1 - P(r)\big)$$

Because $P(r) \in (0, 1)$ for all finite $r$, $\psi(r) > 0$ strictly holds everywhere.

**Step 2: Second Derivative and Identification of the Critical Point**  
Differentiating $\psi(r)$ with respect to $r$:

$$\frac{d^2 P}{dr^2} = k \left( \frac{dP}{dr} (1 - P(r)) + P(r) \left(-\frac{dP}{dr}\right) \right) = k \frac{dP}{dr} \big(1 - 2P(r)\big) = k^2 P(r) (1 - P(r)) \big(1 - 2P(r)\big)$$

To find stationary points of $\psi(r)$, set $\frac{d^2 P}{dr^2} = 0$. Since $k^2 P(r)(1 - P(r)) > 0$ for all finite $r$, the condition requires:

$$1 - 2P(r) = 0 \iff P(r) = \frac{1}{2}$$

Substituting the definition of $P(r)$:

$$\frac{1}{1 + e^{-k(r - r^*)}} = \frac{1}{2} \iff e^{-k(r - r^*)} = 1 \iff -k(r - r^*) = 0 \iff r = r^*$$

Thus, $r = r^*$ is the unique stationary point on $\mathbb{R}$.

**Step 3: Verification of the Strict Global Maximum**  
Evaluating the third derivative of $P(r)$ (the second derivative of $\psi(r)$) at $r = r^*$:

$$\frac{d^3 P}{dr^3} = \frac{d}{dr} \left[ k \frac{dP}{dr} (1 - 2P) \right] = k \frac{d^2 P}{dr^2} (1 - 2P) + k \frac{dP}{dr} \left(-2 \frac{dP}{dr}\right) = k \frac{d^2 P}{dr^2} (1 - 2P) - 2k \left(\frac{dP}{dr}\right)^2$$

At $r = r^*$, $1 - 2P(r^*) = 0$, causing the first term to vanish:

$$\left.\frac{d^3 P}{dr^3}\right|_{r=r^*} = -2k \left(\left.\frac{dP}{dr}\right|_{r=r^*}\right)^2 = -2k \left( k \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \right)^2 = -2k \left(\frac{k}{4}\right)^2 = -\frac{k^3}{8} < 0$$

Because $\left.\frac{d^2 \psi}{dr^2}\right|_{r=r^*} = -\frac{k^3}{8} < 0$, the first derivative $\psi(r) = \frac{dP}{dr}$ achieves a strict global maximum at $r = r^*$.

**Step 4: Maximization of Finite Grant Extensions**  
For a finite grant yielding runway extension $\Delta r > 0$, the total survival lift is:

$$\Delta P(\Delta r, r) = P(r + \Delta r) - P(r) = \int_{r}^{r + \Delta r} \psi(x) \, dx$$

To find the maximizing starting runway $r$, differentiate $\Delta P$ with respect to $r$ using the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:

$$\frac{\partial \Delta P}{\partial r} = \psi(r + \Delta r) - \psi(r)$$

Setting $\frac{\partial \Delta P}{\partial r} = 0$ requires:

$$\psi(r + \Delta r) = \psi(r)$$

Recalling that $\psi(x) = k P(x)(1 - P(x))$ is strictly symmetric about $r^*$ because:

$$P(r^* + \delta) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-k\delta}}, \quad 1 - P(r^* + \delta) = \frac{e^{-k\delta}}{1 + e^{-k\delta}} \implies \psi(r^* + \delta) = k \frac{e^{-k\delta}}{\left(1 + e^{-k\delta}\right)^2}$$
$$P(r^* - \delta) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{k\delta}} = \frac{e^{-k\delta}}{1 + e^{-k\delta}}, \quad 1 - P(r^* - \delta) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-k\delta}} \implies \psi(r^* - \delta) = k \frac{e^{-k\delta}}{\left(1 + e^{-k\delta}\right)^2} = \psi(r^* + \delta)$$

Since $\psi(x)$ is unimodal and strictly symmetric about $r^*$, the equality $\psi(r + \Delta r) = \psi(r)$ for $\Delta r > 0$ holds if and only if the points $r$ and $r + \Delta r$ are equidistant from the center $r^*$:

$$r^* - r = (r + \Delta r) - r^* \iff 2r^* = 2r + \Delta r \iff r_{\max} = r^* - \frac{\Delta r}{2}$$

Evaluating for empirical Central Market parameters where $r^* = 1.0$ and $\Delta r \in [0.5, 1.0]$:

$$r_{\max} \in \left[1.0 - \frac{1.0}{2}, 1.0 - \frac{0.5}{2}\right] = [0.50, 0.75] \subset [0.5, 1.5]$$

This establishes that marginal survival lift is strictly maximized when the applicant operates in the viable paycheck-to-paycheck corridor $r_i \in [0.5, 1.5]$. $\blacksquare$

### 2.4 Asymptotic Properties: The Self-Insuring Decay & Insolvency Trap

#### Asymptotic Limit 1: Self-Insuring Firms ($r_i \ge 4.0 - 6.0$ Quarters)
Consider an applicant with substantial cash reserves, such that $r_i \gg r^*$. For large positive arguments $u = k(r_i - r^*)$, we expand the logistic function:

$$P_0(r_i) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-u}} = 1 - e^{-u} + \mathcal{O}\left(e^{-2u}\right)$$

Under grant extension $\Delta r_i$:

$$P(r_i + \Delta r_i) = 1 - e^{-u - k\Delta r_i} + \mathcal{O}\left(e^{-2(u + k\Delta r_i)}\right)$$

The marginal survival lift is:

$$\Delta P(g_i, r_i) = e^{-k(r_i - r^*)} \left(1 - e^{-k\Delta r_i}\right) + \mathcal{O}\left(e^{-2k(r_i - r^*)}\right)$$

As $r_i \to \infty$, the survival lift decays exponentially:

$$\lim_{r_i \to \infty} \Delta P(g_i, r_i) = 0 \quad \text{at rate } \mathcal{O}\left(e^{-k r_i}\right)$$

*Numerical Evaluation*: For $k = 2.0, r^* = 1.0$, and an award providing $\Delta r = 0.75$:
- At $r_i = 4.0$ quarters: $\Delta P \approx e^{-6.0} (1 - e^{-1.5}) = 0.00247 \times 0.7769 = +0.00192$ ($+0.19\%$ lift).
- At $r_i = 6.0$ quarters: $\Delta P \approx e^{-10.0} (1 - e^{-1.5}) = 0.000045 \times 0.7769 = +0.000035$ ($+0.0035\%$ lift).

**Policy Implication**: Deploying relief dollars to firms with 4 to 6 quarters of runway produces near-zero incremental survival. It subsidizes private savings rather than preventing closure, constituting pure deadweight loss.

#### Asymptotic Limit 2: Terminal Insolvency Trap ($r_i \to 0$ with Massive Burn $\kappa_i \gg R_i$)
Consider an applicant entering liquidation ($r_i \approx 0.05$) whose quarterly operating deficit vastly outstrips available grant resources (e.g., quarterly burn $\kappa_i = \$80,000$ against a maximum micro-grant $R_i = \$10,000$).

The achievable runway extension is minimal:

$$\Delta r_i = \frac{\$10,000}{\$80,000} = 0.125 \text{ quarters (approximately 11 days)}$$

Evaluating survival probabilities:

$$P_0(0.05) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(-2.0(0.05 - 1.0)\right)} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{1.90}} \approx \frac{1}{1 + 6.6859} \approx 0.1301$$
$$P(0.05 + 0.125) = P(0.175) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(-2.0(0.175 - 1.0)\right)} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{1.65}} \approx \frac{1}{1 + 5.2070} \approx 0.1611$$
$$\Delta P = 0.1611 - 0.1301 = +0.0310 \quad (+3.10\%)$$

Despite receiving the maximum grant, the firm faces an $83.9\%$ probability of closure within the year. The $\$10,000$ public investment provides an 11-day delay of liquidation, failing to establish operational self-sufficiency.

### 3.2 Sector Macroeconomic Volatility Metric Formulation

We formalize sector vulnerability via the **Sector Macroeconomic Volatility Index** $\Omega_s \in [0.5, 2.5]$:

$$\Omega_s = \left( \frac{\epsilon_{d, s}}{\max(0.02, \mu_s)} \right) \cdot \sigma_{\text{macro}} \cdot \left(1 + \frac{\tau_{\text{rec}, s}}{4}\right)$$

where:
- $\epsilon_{d, s} = \left| \frac{\partial \ln Q_s}{\partial \ln Y} \right|$ is the sector's income demand elasticity.
- $\mu_s \in (0, 0.25]$ is the corridor median operating profit margin for sector $s$.
- $\sigma_{\text{macro}} \in [0.10, 0.50]$ represents the localized corridor shock intensity (e.g., footfall contraction from remote work, commercial office vacancy rate; default $\sigma_{\text{macro}} = 0.25$).
- $\tau_{\text{rec}, s}$ is the expected recovery horizon in quarters required for sector customer demand to stabilize following a downturn.

---

### 3.3 Acute Liquidity Shock vs. Secular Structural Decline

A critical flaw in standard municipal grant making is mistaking a **temporary liquidity freeze** (which a \$10,000 grant resolves) for **secular structural obsolescence** (where long-term market demand has vanished, rendering public grants ineffective).

To separate acute cyclical distress from permanent decline, we define the **Cyclical Viability Factor** $\Psi_s \in [0, 1]$:

$$\Psi_s = \left( \frac{\text{Baseline Margin Trend}_s}{\text{Downturn Margin Compression}_s + \epsilon} \right) \cdot (1 - \delta_{\text{secular}, s})$$

where $\delta_{\text{secular}, s} \in [0, 1]$ measures technological or structural displacement (e.g., commercial print shops facing digital transition exhibit $\delta_{\text{secular}} \approx 0.80$, whereas artisan food manufacturing and local dining exhibit $\delta_{\text{secular}} \le 0.05$).

---

### 3.4 Macro-Volatility Risk Adjustment Multiplier

The **Sector Risk-Adjustment Multiplier** $\rho_s \in [0.70, 1.35]$ applied to an applicant's priority utility is:

$$\rho_s = 1.0 + \omega_{\text{vol}} \cdot \left[ \left(\frac{\Omega_s - \bar{\Omega}}{\sigma_\Omega}\right) \cdot \Psi_s \right]$$

where:
- $\bar{\Omega}$ and $\sigma_\Omega$ are the mean and standard deviation of volatility indices across all corridor applicant sectors.
- $\omega_{\text{vol}} \in [0, 0.50]$ is the policy weight assigned to sector volatility by the Grant Officer (default $\omega_{\text{vol}} = 0.25$).

#### Mathematical Properties of $\rho_s$:
1. **Targeted Bridge Boost**: Highly volatile but structurally viable sectors (e.g., independent dining, live music venues) receive an upward multiplier ($\rho_s \in [1.15, 1.30]$) because public capital prevents preventable insolvencies during temporary demand freezes.
2. **Secular Decline Neutralization**: Sectors undergoing terminal obsolescence ($\delta_{\text{secular}} \to 1 \implies \Psi_s \to 0$) receive $\rho_s \approx 1.00$, preventing policy dollars from artificially subsidizing obsolete business models.
3. **Inelastic Anchor Protection**: Inelastic essential sectors (grocery, outpatient clinics) have $\Omega_s < \bar{\Omega}$, yielding $\rho_s \in [0.75, 0.90]$. This does not penalize them; rather, it reflects that their priority is correctly established via **Dimension 3 (People Helped & Neighborhood Need)** rather than macroeconomic volatility buffering.

### 4.2 Component Formulations: Direct, Network, Commuter, and Resident

#### 1. Direct Employees Retained ($W_i$)
The direct payroll headcount measured in Full-Time Equivalents (FTE):

$$W_i = \text{FTE}_{\text{full-time}} + 0.50 \cdot \text{PTE}_{\text{part-time}}$$

Direct payroll preservation protects immediate household income for workers employed directly within the corridor.

#### 2. Network-Protected Workers Across Upstream and Downstream Tiers ($W_{\text{network}}(i)$)
When an intermediate supplier survives, it protects jobs across connected downstream customer storefronts $\mathcal{D}(i)$ and upstream vendor businesses $\mathcal{U}(i)$:

$$W_{\text{network}}(i) = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{D}(i)} \pi_{ij} \cdot W_j + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{U}(i)} \pi_{mi} \cdot W_m$$

where $\pi_{ij} \in [0, 1]$ is the **Inter-Firm Dependency Coupling Coefficient**:

$$\pi_{ij} = \frac{\alpha_{ij}}{\sqrt{r(j, \text{type}(i))}}$$

- $\alpha_{ij} \in [0, 1]$ represents the proportion of buyer $j$'s operational input requirements sourced from supplier $i$.
- $r(j, \text{type}(i)) \ge 1$ is the local supplier redundancy (the number of viable alternative suppliers of that input category within a 1.2-kilometer radius). If buyer $j$ has zero alternative local suppliers ($r = 1$), $\pi_{ij} = \alpha_{ij}$. If 4 local competitors exist, coupling is halved ($1/\sqrt{4} = 0.50$).

#### 3. Daily Foot-Traffic Patrons and Commuters ($C_i$)
The daily volume of transit riders, downtown office commuters, and visitors utilizing the storefront:

$$C_i = \Phi_{\text{commute}} \cdot \sum_{b \in \mathcal{B}_{500}(i)} \text{Employment}_b \cdot \gamma_{\text{gravity}}(d_{ib})$$

where:
- $\mathcal{B}_{500}(i)$ is the set of commercial employment blocks within a 500-meter walking shed of storefront $i$.
- $\text{Employment}_b$ is the daytime worker count in block $b$.
- $\gamma_{\text{gravity}}(d_{ib}) = \frac{1}{1 + (d_{ib} / 250)^2}$ is the spatial footfall gravity decay over distance $d_{ib}$ in meters.
- $\Phi_{\text{commute}} \in [0.08, 0.15]$ is the empirical corridor retail conversion capture rate.

#### 4. Vulnerable Car-Free & Low-Income Neighborhood Residents ($R_i$)
The low-income residential population that relies upon the storefront for non-substitutable daily survival needs (e.g., fresh groceries, infant formula, prescription medication, harm reduction services):

$$R_i = \text{Pop}(\text{Tract}_i) \cdot \Big( \text{PovertyRate}_i \cdot \text{NoVehicleRate}_i \Big) \cdot \frac{K_{\text{essential}}(i)}{N_{\text{corridor}}(\text{naics}_i)}$$

where:
- $\text{Pop}(\text{Tract}_i)$ is the total resident population of applicant $i$'s Census tract.
- $\text{PovertyRate}_i$ and $\text{NoVehicleRate}_i$ are the tract's poverty rate and zero-vehicle household rate.
- $K_{\text{essential}}(i) \in [1, 5]$ is the **Essential Good Tiering Weight**:
  - $K_{\text{essential}} = 5$: Fresh Grocery (`NAICS 4451`), Prescription Pharmacy (`4461`), Primary Care Health Clinic (`6214`).
  - $K_{\text{essential}} = 3$: Childcare / Social Support (`624`), Laundromat / Hardware (`8123`, `4441`).
  - $K_{\text{essential}} = 1$: Full-Service Dining / Nightlife (`7225`), Personal Grooming (`8121`).
- $N_{\text{corridor}}(\text{naics}_i) \ge 1$ is the number of operating substitute storefronts in the corridor. When a grocery store is the **sole remaining food provider** on a low-vehicle block ($N_{\text{corridor}} = 1$), $R_i$ expands to encompass the entire vulnerable tract population.

## 5. Dimension 4: Small-Business Purity & Micro-Enterprise Priority

### 5.1 Corporate & Non-Storefront Masquerade in Public Data

A primary empirical vulnerability of public business registries and national input-output tables is that **raw administrative data cannot be ingested without a corporate screening gate**. In public records, national technology corporations, venture-backed mobility firms, biotech research entities, and multi-facility parking operators register local addresses that mimic Main Street small businesses.

Due to broad 2-digit industry rollups (e.g., Code `81` aggregating shoe repair with national parking structures, Code `325` merging industrial cleaning soaps with pharmaceutical biotech), naive graph algorithms assign massive centrality scores to corporate entities while penalizing legitimate micro-enterprises.

---

### 5.2 Corporate Exclusion Gate Predicate

We define the binary **Corporate Exclusion Gate Predicate** $E_{\text{corp}}(i) \in \{0, 1\}$:

$$E_{\text{corp}}(i) = \begin{cases} 
0 & \text{if } \text{Ineligible Corporate / Non-Storefront Entity (DISQUALIFIED)} \\
1 & \text{if } \text{Eligible Main Street Small Business / Micro-Enterprise}
\end{cases}$$

An applicant is disqualified ($E_{\text{corp}}(i) = 0$) if and only if **any** of the following conditions is satisfied:

$$\begin{aligned}
1. & \quad \text{Corporate Technology or Biotech Code}: \text{NAICS}_i \in \{3361, 3342, 3254, 5182, 5415\} \land \neg \text{StorefrontCertified}_i \\
2. & \quad \text{Headcount Ceiling}: W_i > 50 \text{ FTE} \\
3. & \quad \text{Multi-Unit Chain Footprint}: L_i > 3 \text{ physical operating locations across California} \\
4. & \quad \text{Institutional Capital Backing}: \text{Ownership}_i \in \{\text{Publicly Traded}, \text{Venture-Backed}, \text{Private Equity}, \text{National Franchise}\} \\
5. & \quad \text{Non-Storefront Aggregator}: \text{Physical Public Streetfront Activation} = \text{False (e.g., automated parking lot)}
\end{aligned}$$

When $E_{\text{corp}}(i) = 0$, the business is permanently diverted to the **Corporate Audit Flag Tray** and receives **\$0.00** from municipal emergency funds.

### 5.3 Continuous Micro-Enterprise Purity Scoring Function

For all eligible applicants ($E_{\text{corp}}(i) = 1$), we evaluate a continuous **Small-Business Purity Score** $P_{\text{pure}}(i) \in (0, 100]$:

$$P_{\text{pure}}(i) = 100 \cdot \Big[ f_{\text{size}}(W_i) \times f_{\text{loc}}(L_i) \times f_{\text{own}}(O_i) \times f_{\text{rev}}(Y_i) \Big]$$

### 5.4 Non-Linear Size and Location Penalties

#### 1. Non-Linear Headcount Function $f_{\text{size}}(W_i)$
The headcount function provides maximum priority to true micro-enterprises (1–5 workers) and imposes steep exponential decay for larger entities:

$$f_{\text{size}}(W_i) = \begin{cases} 
1.00 & \text{if } W_i \le 5 \quad \text{(Micro-Enterprise Core)} \\
1.00 - 0.40 \left(\frac{W_i - 5}{15}\right) & \text{if } 5 < W_i \le 20 \quad \text{(Standard Small Business)} \\
0.60 \cdot \exp\left(-\beta_{\text{head}} (W_i - 20)\right) & \text{if } 20 < W_i \le 50 \quad \text{(Upper Small Business)}
\end{cases}$$

with steepness parameter $\beta_{\text{head}} = 0.05$. At $W = 5$, $f_{\text{size}} = 1.00$; at $W = 20$, $f_{\text{size}} = 0.60$; at $W = 50$, $f_{\text{size}} \approx 0.134$.

#### 2. Operating Locations Multiplier $f_{\text{loc}}(L_i)$
$$f_{\text{loc}}(L_i) = \begin{cases} 
1.00 & \text{if } L_i = 1 \quad \text{(Single Independent Storefront)} \\
0.70 & \text{if } L_i = 2 \quad \text{(Two Local Locations)} \\
0.40 & \text{if } L_i = 3 \quad \text{(Three Regional Locations)} \\

#### 3. Ownership Structure Multiplier $f_{\text{own}}(O_i)$

0.60 & \text{Regional Partnership} \\
0.20 & \text{Corporate Subcontractor / Franchise Licensee}
\end{cases}$$

#### 4. Gross Annual Revenue Multiplier $f_{\text{rev}}(Y_i)$

where $Y_i$ is gross annual revenue. Micro-enterprises earning under $\$300,000$ maintain $f_{\text{rev}} \ge 0.80$, while businesses grossing above $\$2,500,000$ see $f_{\text{rev}}$ decay below $0.25$.

## 6. Dimension 5: Parent / Root Supplier Hierarchical Importance

### 6.1 Topological Asymmetry: Root Suppliers vs. Leaf Nodes

In a localized urban commercial graph, node roles are structurally asymmetric. A retail restaurant or convenience bodega represents a **terminal leaf node**: it consumes intermediate goods but sells almost exclusively to final consumer demand ($k_{\text{out}} \approx 0$).

Conversely, a **foundational parent or root supplier**—such as an artisan wholesale production bakery (*Sour Flour*) or specialty coffee roasting commissary (*Cumaica Coffee*)—occupies an upstream root position:

```
               [Root Supplier: Sour Flour / Cumaica Coffee]
                                    │
         ┌──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┐
         ▼                          ▼                          ▼
   [Downstream Cafe A]       [Downstream Diner B]       [Downstream Grocer C]
   (5-8 Employees)           (6-10 Employees)           (4-7 Employees)
         │                          │                          │
         ▼                          ▼                          ▼
   [Final Patrons]            [Final Patrons]            [Final Residents]
```

- If a single **leaf cafe** closes, the loss is localized: upstream producers lose a fraction of intermediate demand, but their remaining 40–80 buyers maintain operations.
- If the **root supplier** closes, every downstream restaurant and cafe that relied on that supplier experiences an immediate input shock. In Central Market, where only two commercial food manufacturers operate, substitution friction is severe: downstream buyers face product degradation, high distributor minimums, or secondary closure.

---

### 6.2 Upstream Asymmetry Centrality Formulation

We formalize upstream status via the directed **Upstream Asymmetry Centrality Metric** $\xi_i \in [0, \infty)$:

$$\xi_i = \left( \frac{k_{\text{out}}(i) + 1}{k_{\text{in}}(i) + 1} \right) \cdot \sum_{j \in \mathcal{D}_1(i)} \frac{\alpha_{ij}}{\sqrt{r(j, \text{type}(i))}} + \lambda_{\text{hop}} \sum_{m \in \mathcal{D}_2(i)} \frac{\alpha_{im} \cdot \alpha_{mj}}{\sqrt{r(m, \text{type}(i))}}$$

where:
- $k_{\text{out}}(i)$ and $k_{\text{in}}(i)$ are the out-degree (commercial buyers supplied) and in-degree (commercial vendors utilized).
- For a root commissary supplying 85 cafes and purchasing commodity grains: $k_{\text{out}} = 85, k_{\text{in}} = 2 \implies \text{Degree Ratio} = \frac{86}{3} \approx 28.67$.
- For a leaf restaurant purchasing from 8 vendors and selling to consumers: $k_{\text{out}} = 0, k_{\text{in}} = 8 \implies \text{Degree Ratio} = \frac{1}{9} \approx 0.11$.
- The topological ratio spans over **two orders of magnitude** ($>250\times$ difference), isolating foundational producers.

---

### 6.3 Cascading Failure Multiplier Formulation

We define the **Cascading Failure Multiplier** $\mu_i \ge 1.0$:

$$\mu_i = 1.0 + \phi_{\text{parent}} \cdot \ln\left(1 + \xi_i\right)$$

where $\phi_{\text{parent}} \in [0, 0.75]$ is the **Parent Supplier Weight Slider** set by the Grant Officer (default $\phi_{\text{parent}} = 0.35$).

#### Mathematical Properties of $\mu_i$:
1. **Leaf Node Invariance**: For pure consumer storefronts ($k_{\text{out}} = 0$), $\xi_i \approx 0 \implies \ln(1 + 0) = 0 \implies \mu_i = 1.00$. Leaf nodes retain their complete intrinsic score without artificial down-weighting.
2. **Logarithmic Sub-Linear Growth**: Scaling with $\ln(1 + \xi_i)$ ensures that large wholesale producers receive meaningful priority ($\mu_i \approx 1.80 - 2.25$) without completely eclipsing neighborhood retail anchors.

---

### 6.4 Theorem 2 & Formal Proof: Upstream Cascading Loss Amplification

#### Statement of Theorem 2
*Let $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ be a directed commercial supply network where failure propagation probability from supplier $u$ to buyer $v$ is given by $p_{uv} = \pi_{uv} (1 - \sigma_v)$, with coupling $\pi_{uv} > 0$ and buyer substitution capacity $\sigma_v \in [0, 1)$. Suppose applicant $u$ is a root supplier with out-degree $K \ge 1$ and downstream buyers $\{v_1, \dots, v_K\}$, while applicant $w$ is an isolated leaf node with identical direct output $V_{\text{direct}}(u) = V_{\text{direct}}(w) = V_0$. Then the total expected corridor ecosystem loss from the insolvency of root supplier $u$ strictly exceeds the loss from the failure of leaf $w$ by at least:*

$$\mathbb{E}[\Delta \mathcal{W}_{\text{loss}}(u)] - \mathbb{E}[\Delta \mathcal{W}_{\text{loss}}(w)] \ge \sum_{j=1}^K p_{uj} \cdot V_{\text{direct}}(v_j) - \sum_{m \in \mathcal{U}(w)} \alpha_{mw} V_{\text{direct}}(m)$$

*and when downstream substitute redundancy is scarce ($r \approx 1$) and $K \gg 1$, this systemic disparity is strictly positive and scales linearly with $K$.*

#### Formal Mathematical Proof

**Step 1: Expected Loss from Leaf Failure**  
Consider the insolvency event $\mathcal{F}_w$ of leaf applicant $w$. Because $w$ sells exclusively to final consumers, its out-degree is zero:

$$\mathcal{D}(w) = \emptyset \implies k_{\text{out}}(w) = 0$$

No downstream business experiences an input shock. The failure impacts only $w$ directly, plus backward demand reductions to its upstream suppliers $m \in \mathcal{U}(w)$:

$$\mathbb{E}[\Delta \mathcal{W}_{\text{loss}}(w)] = V_{\text{direct}}(w) + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{U}(w)} \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F}_m \mid \mathcal{F}_w) \cdot V_{\text{direct}}(m)$$

Backward demand failure is bounded by the supplier's reliance on buyer $w$: $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F}_m \mid \mathcal{F}_w) \le \alpha_{mw}$. Because a supplier serves dozens of accounts, $\alpha_{mw} \ll 1$. Thus:

$$\mathbb{E}[\Delta \mathcal{W}_{\text{loss}}(w)] \le V_0 + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{U}(w)} \alpha_{mw} V_{\text{direct}}(m)$$

**Step 2: Expected Loss from Root Supplier Failure**  
Consider the insolvency event $\mathcal{F}_u$ of root supplier $u$. The failure triggers the direct loss of $u$, $V_{\text{direct}}(u) = V_0$. In addition, each downstream buyer $v_j \in \mathcal{D}_1(u)$ suffers an induced operational failure probability:

$$\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F}_{v_j} \mid \mathcal{F}_u) = p_{uj} = \pi_{uj} (1 - \sigma_{v_j}) = \frac{\alpha_{uj}}{\sqrt{r(v_j, \text{type}(u))}} (1 - \sigma_{v_j})$$

By linearity of expectation, the expected first-order downstream failure loss is:

$$\mathbb{E}[\text{Downstream Loss}_1] = \sum_{j=1}^K \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{F}_{v_j} \mid \mathcal{F}_u) \cdot V_{\text{direct}}(v_j) = \sum_{j=1}^K p_{uj} V_{\text{direct}}(v_j)$$

Furthermore, each failed downstream buyer $v_j$ triggers second-order induced losses across its own buyers $k \in \mathcal{D}_1(v_j)$:

$$\mathbb{E}[\text{Downstream Loss}_2] = \sum_{j=1}^K \sum_{k \in \mathcal{D}_1(v_j)} p_{uj} p_{jk} V_{\text{direct}}(k) \ge 0$$

Thus, the total expected loss is:

$$\mathbb{E}[\Delta \mathcal{W}_{\text{loss}}(u)] = V_0 + \sum_{j=1}^K p_{uj} V_{\text{direct}}(v_j) + \mathbb{E}[\text{Downstream Loss}_2] + \sum_{m \in \mathcal{U}(u)} \alpha_{mu} V_{\text{direct}}(m)$$

**Step 3: Difference and Asymptotic Evaluation**  
Subtracting the leaf loss expectation:

$$\mathbb{E}[\Delta \mathcal{W}_{\text{loss}}(u)] - \mathbb{E}[\Delta \mathcal{W}_{\text{loss}}(w)] \ge \sum_{j=1}^K p_{uj} V_{\text{direct}}(v_j) - \sum_{m \in \mathcal{U}(w)} \alpha_{mw} V_{\text{direct}}(m)$$

In Central Market, root suppliers like *Sour Flour* and *Cumaica Coffee* supply $K \ge 85$ independent dining establishments. For each buyer, local artisan redundancy is minimal ($r = 1 \implies \pi_{uj} = \alpha_{uj} \approx 0.20 - 0.35$). Even with moderate substitution buffer ($\sigma \approx 0.50$), $p_{uj} \ge 0.10$.

Assuming median buyer economic value $\bar{V}_{\text{down}}$:

$$\sum_{j=1}^{85} p_{uj} V_{\text{direct}}(v_j) \ge 85 \times (0.10) \times \bar{V}_{\text{down}} = 8.5 \cdot \bar{V}_{\text{down}}$$

Conversely, the backward loss of leaf node $w$ across its 3 suppliers is:

$$\sum_{m=1}^3 \alpha_{mw} V_{\text{direct}}(m) \le 3 \times (0.05) \times \bar{V}_{\text{up}} = 0.15 \cdot \bar{V}_{\text{up}}$$

Therefore:

$$\mathbb{E}[\Delta \mathcal{W}_{\text{loss}}(u)] - \mathbb{E}[\Delta \mathcal{W}_{\text{loss}}(w)] \ge 8.5 \bar{V}_{\text{down}} - 0.15 \bar{V}_{\text{up}} > 0$$

The systemic damage resulting from root supplier insolvency exceeds leaf failure by over **$50\times$**. Hence, assigning an explicit upstream multiplier $\mu_i > 1$ is mathematically mandatory to minimize expected corridor economic destruction. $\blacksquare$

## 7. Unified Portfolio Knapsack Formulation & Allocation Regimes

### 7.1 Unified Expected Marginal Ecosystem Utility Function

We unite all five beyond-dataset real-world factors with baseline network cascade and neighborhood equity scores into a single **Expected Marginal Ecosystem Utility Function** $U_i(g_i, \vec{w})$:

$$U_i(g_i, \vec{w}) = E_{\text{corp}}(i) \cdot \Delta P(g_i, r_i) \cdot \rho_{s(i)} \cdot \mu_i \cdot \Gamma_i(\vec{w}) \cdot M_{\text{equity}}(i)$$

where $\Gamma_i(\vec{w})$ is the **Multi-Criteria Core Value**:

$$\Gamma_i(\vec{w}) = w_{\text{casc}} \cdot S_{\text{cascade}}(i) + w_{\text{anch}} \cdot S_{\text{anchor}}(i) + w_{\text{peop}} \cdot S_{\text{people}}(i) + w_{\text{pure}} \cdot P_{\text{pure}}(i)$$

subject to the policy simplex constraint:

$$\vec{w} = (w_{\text{casc}}, w_{\text{anch}}, w_{\text{peop}}, w_{\text{pure}}) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^4, \quad \sum w_k = 1.0$$

Baseline policy weights:

$$w_{\text{casc}} = 0.30, \quad w_{\text{anch}} = 0.25, \quad w_{\text{peop}} = 0.25, \quad w_{\text{pure}} = 0.20$$

---

### 7.2 Submodular Downstream Overlap Deduplication

A recognized defect in standard knapsack optimization is treating applicants as mathematically independent. In a dense corridor, multiple suppliers often serve the **same downstream buyers**.

In Central Market, *Sour Flour* and *Cumaica Coffee* share over 60 common downstream wholesale buyers. Funding *Sour Flour* protects those shared cafes against bakery input failure. If *Cumaica Coffee* is also funded, the marginal protection afforded to those identical shared buyers is subject to diminishing returns.

Let $\mathcal{S} \subseteq \mathcal{Q}$ denote the set of funded applicants. We formulate portfolio value as a **submodular set function**:

$$\mathcal{U}_{\text{portfolio}}(\mathcal{S}) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{S}} U_i(g_i) - \lambda_{\text{overlap}} \sum_{\substack{i, j \in \mathcal{S} \\ i < j}} J(\mathcal{D}_i, \mathcal{D}_j) \cdot \min(U_i, U_j)$$

where:
- $\mathcal{D}_i$ is the set of downstream commercial buyers served by applicant $i$.
- $J(\mathcal{D}_i, \mathcal{D}_j) = \frac{|\mathcal{D}_i \cap \mathcal{D}_j|}{|\mathcal{D}_i \cup \mathcal{D}_j|}$ is the Jaccard similarity index of downstream customer overlap.
- $\lambda_{\text{overlap}} \in [0, 0.50]$ is the overlap penalty weight (default $\lambda_{\text{overlap}} = 0.25$).

---

### 7.3 Constrained Portfolio Knapsack Optimization

The Grant Officer’s decision is formalized as a **Constrained Submodular Knapsack Optimization Problem**:

$$\max_{\{g_i\}_{i \in \mathcal{Q}}} \quad \mathcal{U}_{\text{portfolio}}\big(\{i \mid g_i > 0\}\big)$$

subject to:

## 8. Analytical Theorems, Monotonicity Proofs, & Robustness Bounds

### 8.1 Theorem 3 & Formal Proof: Strict Monotonicity in Survival Lift

#### Statement of Theorem 3
*For any eligible applicant $i$ ($E_{\text{corp}}(i) = 1$) with fixed positive ecosystem attributes ($\rho_s > 0, \mu_i > 0, \Gamma_i > 0, M_{\text{equity}} > 0$), and for any fixed grant award $g_i > 0$, the composite objective utility $U_i(g_i, r_i)$ is strictly decreasing with respect to baseline operational runway $r_i$ for all $r_i \ge r^* = 1.0$:*

$$\frac{\partial U_i}{\partial r_i} < 0 \quad \forall r_i \ge 1.0$$

#### Formal Mathematical Proof
From the definition of composite utility in Section 7.1:

$$U_i(g_i, r_i) = C_0 \cdot \Delta P(g_i, r_i)$$

where $C_0 = E_{\text{corp}}(i) \cdot \rho_{s(i)} \cdot \mu_i \cdot \Gamma_i(\vec{w}) \cdot M_{\text{equity}}(i) > 0$ is a strictly positive constant independent of $r_i$.

Differentiating with respect to baseline runway $r_i$:

$$\frac{\partial U_i}{\partial r_i} = C_0 \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial r_i} \Big[ P(r_i + \Delta r_i) - P(r_i) \Big] = C_0 \cdot \Big[ P'(r_i + \Delta r_i) - P'(r_i) \Big]$$

From Theorem 1, the derivative $P'(r) = \psi(r) = k P(r)(1 - P(r))$ satisfies:

$$\frac{d \psi}{dr} = \frac{d^2 P}{dr^2} = k^2 P(r)(1 - P(r))(1 - 2P(r))$$

For all $r > r^* = 1.0$, $P(r) > 0.50$, which implies $1 - 2P(r) < 0$.  
Since $k^2 P(r)(1 - P(r)) > 0$, we have:

$$\frac{d \psi}{dr} < 0 \quad \forall r > r^*$$

Thus, $\psi(r) = P'(r)$ is strictly monotonically decreasing on $[r^*, \infty)$.

Because $r_i \ge r^*$ and $\Delta r_i > 0$, it follows that $r_i + \Delta r_i > r_i \ge r^*$. The strict monotonicity of $P'(r)$ guarantees:

$$P'(r_i + \Delta r_i) < P'(r_i)$$

Therefore:

$$P'(r_i + \Delta r_i) - P'(r_i) < 0$$

Multiplying by $C_0 > 0$ yields:

$$\frac{\partial U_i}{\partial r_i} < 0 \quad \forall r_i \ge 1.0$$

This proves that increasing baseline cash reserves above 1 quarter strictly reduces emergency grant priority. $\blacksquare$

### 8.2 Theorem 4 & Formal Proof: Strict Monotonicity in Small-Business Purity

#### Statement of Theorem 4
*Let applicants $A$ and $B$ have identical operational runway ($r_A = r_B$), identical sector volatility ($\rho_A = \rho_B$), identical supplier centrality ($\mu_A = \mu_B$), and identical network/anchor/people metrics ($S_{\text{casc}, A} = S_{\text{casc}, B}$, $S_{\text{anch}, A} = S_{\text{anch}, B}$, $S_{\text{peop}, A} = S_{\text{peop}, B}$). If applicant $A$ is an independent single-location micro-enterprise ($L_A = 1, W_A \le 5, O_A = \text{Indep}$) and applicant $B$ is a multi-unit regional enterprise ($L_B = 3, W_B = 20, O_B = \text{Regional}$), then for any policy weight vector $\vec{w}$ with $w_{\text{pure}} > 0$:*

$$U_A(g, \vec{w}) > U_B(g, \vec{w})$$

#### Formal Mathematical Proof
From Section 5.3, the purity scoring function is:

$$P_{\text{pure}}(i) = 100 \cdot \Big[ f_{\text{size}}(W_i) \cdot f_{\text{loc}}(L_i) \cdot f_{\text{own}}(O_i) \cdot f_{\text{rev}}(Y_i) \Big]$$

Evaluating for applicant $A$:
- $W_A \le 5 \implies f_{\text{size}}(W_A) = 1.00$
- $L_A = 1 \implies f_{\text{loc}}(L_A) = 1.00$
- $O_A = \text{Indep} \implies f_{\text{own}}(O_A) = 1.00$
- $P_{\text{pure}}(A) = 100 \cdot (1.00 \times 1.00 \times 1.00) \cdot f_{\text{rev}}(Y_A) = 100 \cdot f_{\text{rev}}(Y_A)$

Evaluating for applicant $B$:
- $W_B = 20 \implies f_{\text{size}}(W_B) = 1.00 - 0.40(15/15) = 0.60$
- $L_B = 3 \implies f_{\text{loc}}(L_B) = 0.40$
- $O_B = \text{Regional} \implies f_{\text{own}}(O_B) = 0.60$
- $P_{\text{pure}}(B) = 100 \cdot (0.60 \times 0.40 \times 0.60) \cdot f_{\text{rev}}(Y_B) = 14.4 \cdot f_{\text{rev}}(Y_B)$

Since revenue scaling satisfies $f_{\text{rev}}(Y_A) \ge f_{\text{rev}}(Y_B) > 0$, we establish:

$$P_{\text{pure}}(A) \ge \frac{100}{14.4} P_{\text{pure}}(B) \approx 6.94 \cdot P_{\text{pure}}(B) > P_{\text{pure}}(B)$$

Now evaluate the multi-criteria value cores $\Gamma_A$ and $\Gamma_B$:

$$\Gamma_A(\vec{w}) - \Gamma_B(\vec{w}) = w_{\text{pure}} \cdot \Big( P_{\text{pure}}(A) - P_{\text{pure}}(B) \Big)$$

Because $w_{\text{pure}} > 0$ and $P_{\text{pure}}(A) - P_{\text{pure}}(B) > 0$, we have $\Gamma_A(\vec{w}) > \Gamma_B(\vec{w})$.

Since all other multiplicative factors in $U_i$ are identical and strictly positive:

$$U_A(g, \vec{w}) - U_B(g, \vec{w}) = C_1 \cdot \big(\Gamma_A(\vec{w}) - \Gamma_B(\vec{w})\big) > 0$$

where $C_1 = E_{\text{corp}} \cdot \Delta P \cdot \rho_s \cdot \mu \cdot M_{\text{equity}} > 0$. Therefore, $U_A(g, \vec{w}) > U_B(g, \vec{w})$. $\blacksquare$

### 8.4 Theorem 5 & Formal Proof: Robust Awardee Sensitivity Bounds

#### Statement of Theorem 5
*Let $\mathcal{W}$ denote the regulatory policy hypercube of allowable objective weights:*

$$\mathcal{W} = \left\{ \vec{w} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^4 \;\middle|\; \sum_{k=1}^4 w_k = 1.0, \quad w_k^{\min} \le w_k \le w_k^{\max} \; \forall k \in \{\text{casc}, \text{anch}, \text{peop}, \text{pure}\} \right\}$$

*with regulatory bounds $w_k^{\min} = 0.10$ and $w_k^{\max} = 0.50$. Let $\mathcal{S}^*(\vec{w})$ denote the set of funded applicants under portfolio knapsack optimization with budget $B$. Define an applicant $i$ as a **Robust Awardee** if $i \in \mathcal{S}^*(\vec{w})$ for all $\vec{w} \in \mathcal{W}$.*

*If an applicant $i$ satisfies:*

$$\min_{\vec{w} \in \mathcal{V}(\mathcal{W})} U_i(\vec{w}) > \max_{j \in \mathcal{Q} \setminus \mathcal{S}^*} \max_{\vec{w} \in \mathcal{V}(\mathcal{W})} U_j(\vec{w})$$

*where $\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{W})$ is the finite set of extreme vertices of $\mathcal{W}$, then applicant $i$ is guaranteed to remain funded across the entire continuous policy space $\mathcal{W}$.*

#### Formal Mathematical Proof

**Step 1: Linearity of the Core Objective**  
For any applicant $i$, the core value $\Gamma_i(\vec{w}) = \vec{w} \cdot \vec{S}_i$ is a linear inner product of the weight vector $\vec{w}$ with the applicant’s attribute vector:

$$\vec{S}_i = \big(S_{\text{cascade}}(i), S_{\text{anchor}}(i), S_{\text{people}}(i), P_{\text{pure}}(i)\big)^T$$

Because utility $U_i(\vec{w}) = K_i \cdot \Gamma_i(\vec{w})$, where $K_i = E_{\text{corp}}(i) \cdot \Delta P_i \cdot \rho_{s(i)} \cdot \mu_i \cdot M_{\text{equity}}(i) \ge 0$, the function $U_i(\vec{w})$ is an affine (linear) function of $\vec{w}$.

**Step 2: Convexity of the Admissible Weight Space**  
The regulatory hypercube $\mathcal{W}$ is defined by linear equality ($\sum w_k = 1$) and linear inequalities ($w_k^{\min} \le w_k \le w_k^{\max}$). Hence, $\mathcal{W}$ is a compact convex polytope in $\mathbb{R}^4$.

By the Krein-Milman Theorem, any point $\vec{w} \in \mathcal{W}$ can be expressed as a convex combination of its extreme vertices $\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{W}) = \{\vec{v}_1, \dots, \vec{v}_M\}$:

$$\vec{w} = \sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m \vec{v}_m, \quad \lambda_m \ge 0, \quad \sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m = 1$$

**Step 3: Extremum Principle on Polytopes**  
Because $U_i(\vec{w})$ is linear:

$$U_i(\vec{w}) = U_i\left(\sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m \vec{v}_m\right) = \sum_{m=1}^M \lambda_m U_i(\vec{v}_m)$$

The minimum and maximum of an affine function over a compact convex polytope are attained at the extreme vertices:

$$\min_{\vec{w} \in \mathcal{W}} U_i(\vec{w}) = \min_{\vec{v} \in \mathcal{V}(\mathcal{W})} U_i(\vec{v})$$
$$\max_{\vec{w} \in \mathcal{W}} U_j(\vec{w}) = \max_{\vec{v} \in \mathcal{V}(\mathcal{W})} U_j(\vec{v})$$

**Step 4: Strict Separation**  
By hypothesis:

$$\min_{\vec{v} \in \mathcal{V}(\mathcal{W})} U_i(\vec{v}) > \max_{j \notin \mathcal{S}^*} \max_{\vec{v} \in \mathcal{V}(\mathcal{W})} U_j(\vec{v})$$

Therefore, for any arbitrary weight vector $\vec{w}^* \in \mathcal{W}$:

$$U_i(\vec{w}^*) \ge \min_{\vec{v} \in \mathcal{V}(\mathcal{W})} U_i(\vec{v}) > \max_{j \notin \mathcal{S}^*} \max_{\vec{v} \in \mathcal{V}(\mathcal{W})} U_j(\vec{v}) \ge \max_{j \notin \mathcal{S}^*} U_j(\vec{w}^*)$$

Consequently, applicant $i$ maintains a strictly higher utility than any unselected applicant $j$ for every weight vector in $\mathcal{W}$. The applicant is invariant to weight tuning and belongs to the **Robust Awardee Set**. $\blacksquare$
