Central Market Corridor: The $50,000 Micro-Grant Allocation Challenge

San Francisco's Central Market corridor (spanning Market Street from 5th to 10th Streets, intersecting the acute 6th & Mission two-block stretch) represents one of the most economically fragile, socially vital commercial corridors in the nation. Facing commuter foot-traffic reductions, retail closures, and localized street-level crises, municipal grant officers are tasked with deploying scarce emergency capital ($50,000 quarterly tranches) among competing distressed storefronts.

⚠️ How Naive Triage Models Fail

1. Siloed Distress-Only Intake Naivete
Evaluating businesses solely by self-reported intake distress or rent arrears funds isolated, non-viable businesses while allowing foundational local commissaries and bakeries to fail, triggering cascading downstream closures across dozens of independent cafes.
2. Raw Multi-Hop Network Centrality Naivete
Querying raw business registry filings and national input-output tables erroneously routes emergency micro-grants to venture-backed autonomous vehicle fleets (Waymo LLC) and enterprise cloud software providers (WP Engine) while assigning a 0.0 score to 100% of neighborhood grocery stores and clinics.
3. Spatial Blindness & Two-Block Tipping Points
Scoring storefronts as isolated nodes ignores vacancy contagion. On dense blocks like 6th & Mission, funding 1 business while 3 neighbors board up strands the lone survivor in an active commercial dead zone, guaranteeing eventual closure.

✅ Our First-Principles Solution

1. Stage 0 Main-Street Eligibility & Scale Gate
Strict multi-layer filter that disaggregates summary classifications, filters corporate technology/biotech platforms into an Ineligible Drawer, and guarantees $0.00 public leakage to well-capitalized entities.
2. Dual-Mode Queue Triage (Contagion + Essential Access)
Balances path-attenuated supply-chain cascades (protecting upstream food makers) with consumer footfall gravity and essential service scarcity (protecting corner grocers and health clinics in car-free tracts).
3. Beyond-Dataset Real-World Factors & Submodular Allocation
Integrates financial buffer runway (concentrating funds on 0.5–1.5 quarter paycheck-to-paycheck firms), sector volatility, total people helped, small-biz purity, root supplier hierarchy, and deduplicates shared downstream beneficiaries.

⚙️ The 5 Beyond-Dataset Economic Dimensions

1. Financial Buffer Runway (`runway_quarters`)
Models consecutive bad quarters a business can survive before insolvency. Grants are targeted at viable paycheck-to-paycheck businesses (0.5–1.5 quarters) where relief yields maximum marginal survival lift $\Delta P$, while businesses with ≥ 4 quarters of reserves receive minimal priority.
2. Sector Health & Macroeconomic Volatility (`sector_volatility`)
Incorporates margin thickness, demand elasticity, and cyclical shock exposure. Provides temporary stabilization to high-volatility, thin-margin sectors (independent food service, live arts) while distinguishing acute shocks from terminal decline.
3. Maximizing Total People Helped (`people_helped_score`)
Combines direct payroll employees retained, network workers protected across suppliers and buyers, daily pedestrian commuters, and car-free low-income residents who depend on the storefront for essential daily needs.
4. Small-Business Purity & Micro-Enterprise Priority (`small_biz_purity`)
Strictly scores single-location status, family/immigrant ownership, and modest revenue scale, ensuring scarce municipal dollars flow to genuine mom-and-pop storefronts rather than corporate franchises.
5. Parent / Root Supplier Importance (`parent_supplier_weight`)
Applies an upstream multiplier to foundational root producers (commissary bakeries, coffee roasteries) whose failure triggers cascading supply disruptions across dozens of downstream cafes and diners.
